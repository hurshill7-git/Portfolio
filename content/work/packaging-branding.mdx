<Section eyebrow="01 · Context" title="No client, just two things I wanted to get better at">

Not every project here started with a brief. This one started with curiosity: I wanted to get better at two specific things, making a product mockup actually look real (the lighting, the material, the little imperfections that sell it), and designing type I'd actually want to wear.

<InPlainTerms>
There's no client or business problem in this one. It's self-directed practice, one thread in product/packaging mockups, one in typography, both aimed at the same question: does this look like something real, or does it look like a file?
</InPlainTerms>

The packaging side needed generic, ordinary categories, so the render was the whole point, not a brand strategy. The typography side needed something I actually cared about, so I picked Coldplay and let it become a small fan-made print collection.

</Section>

<Section eyebrow="02 · Role & process" title="Two threads, one habit: make it look real">

<ProcessSteps>
  <Step n="01" title="Picked ordinary categories to mock up">A beard-care set, a green tea pouch, a burger bag and coffee cup, each under one consistent personal mark so the render, not the branding, stayed the focus.</Step>
  <Step n="02" title="Explored Coldplay typography">Several wordmark treatments, a guitar built into the letterforms, an English/Devanagari bilingual version, a mascot lockup, before settling on the ones worth using.</Step>
  <Step n="03" title="Built a small print set">Band-portrait pop art, a wings illustration, a tour-poster-style scene, each sized and cropped for tees and hoodies.</Step>
  <Step n="04" title="Composited it onto apparel mockups">Flat and hanger shots first, then a small lookbook-style set, styled like an actual drop.</Step>
</ProcessSteps>

</Section>

<Section eyebrow="03 · Key decisions" title="Choices that came from practicing, not from a client">

<Decision n="01" title="One mark across unrelated categories">

**What.** The same personal wordmark applied to a beard-care bottle, a tea pouch, a burger bag, and a coffee cup, instead of inventing a new brand for each one.

**Why.** The point was practicing render across materials, glass, foil, matte cardboard, waxed paper, not practicing brand strategy. One mark kept that the honest focus.

**Tradeoff.** None of it reads as a coherent brand the way a real client system would, and it isn't meant to.

**Result.** A small, consistent set that's really about the craft of the mockup, not a pitch for an imaginary product line.

</Decision>

<Decision n="02" title="Several wordmarks before picking one">

**What.** Tried a handful of different "Coldplay" logotype treatments, literal, bilingual, mascot-led, rather than settling on the first one that looked fine as a flat logo.

**Why.** A wordmark reads differently at a glance and on fabric than it does sitting flat in a design file. More attempts surfaced which ones actually held up once printed.

**Tradeoff.** Extra throwaway work, some of these never made it onto a shirt.

**Result.** A handful of marks that survived contact with an actual mockup, instead of one that might not have.

</Decision>

<Decision n="03" title="A small lookbook, not just flat mockups">

**What.** Composited a few of the designs onto realistic worn-on-body shots, styled loosely like a real drop.

**Why.** A flat product shot hides scale and placement problems that only show up on a body, in real light, at a real size.

**Tradeoff.** More compositing work for something that's still fan art, not a real release.

**Result.** A set that reads like a finished, small collection instead of a folder of logo files.

</Decision>

</Section>

<Section eyebrow="05 · Impact" title="What I actually got out of it">

There's no client feedback or sales number to report here, that was never the point. What I can point to: a sharper eye for what makes a mockup read as real instead of rendered, several wordmark treatments I'd reuse, and a small, finished print collection I'd genuinely wear. Practice that ends with something you'd actually use is practice that stuck.

</Section>

<Section eyebrow="06 · Reflection" title="Practice needs a real finish line too">

It's tempting to let self-directed work stay loose forever, there's no client waiting, so nothing forces it to actually finish. Treating it like a real collection, complete print set, real mockups, a small lookbook, was what made it useful. The constraint I gave myself (one mark across every packaging category, several wordmarks before picking one) did the same job a client brief usually does: it kept the practice honest instead of just decorative.

</Section>
